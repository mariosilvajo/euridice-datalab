**The brief:** a university wants a model to help decide which applicants
receive a funded study place. You are the analyst. Build the best model you
can *justify*, audit it with the ethics-lab measurements, and write the
**model card** that a regulator, a journalist or an applicant would read.

You make the decisions: which features the model may use (including whether
to give it `has_own_laptop`), which kind of model, and whether it should be
deployed at all. There is no single right answer — what counts is that your
decisions follow from your evidence, and that your documentation is honest
about what the model gets wrong.

The last step **exports your portfolio**: your model card plus every
written judgment from the DataLab's ethics labs, as one file.

## What a strong model card shows

- You know how much your model beats the baseline — and say so plainly.
- The audit is reported as found, including what looks bad.
- The limitations name **who** the model serves poorly, and why.
- The recommendation follows from the evidence above it.
