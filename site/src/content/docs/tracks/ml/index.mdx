**From analysing the past to predicting the future.** This track
demystifies machine learning by having you *do* it: train a real model,
evaluate it honestly, and confront the responsibility that comes with it.

## What you'll be able to do

- Frame a prediction problem: **features** vs **target**
- Split data into training and test sets — and explain *why*
- Train a **decision tree classifier** with scikit-learn
- Read accuracy and a **confusion matrix** without fooling yourself
- **Measure your model's fairness with code** — per-group error rates and
  the four-fifths rule — and place the findings correctly in EU law, which
  imposes duties but prescribes **no fairness metric**
- Spot **overfitting**, choose a model with **cross-validation**, and
  compare models on fairness as well as accuracy
- Write a **model card** and export a **portfolio** of your work

## The dataset

**Student Digital Skills** — 900 learners with their study field, weekly
online-learning hours, digital-skill score and device access; the target is
whether they passed a digital-skills certification. *(Synthetic,
education-sector themed.)*

## Fair warning

Every lesson ends with an **ethics lab**. You will measure your own model
failing learners who can't afford a laptop, discover that the standard
fairness screen misses it, find that a *more accurate* model makes the gap
*wider*, and put your deployment decisions in writing. Those are the most
important cells in the track — and the part no other platform teaches.

The capstone collects those written decisions, plus your own model card,
into a **portfolio** you can download and submit for formal assessment —
see [Earning a microcredential](/credentials/).
