In Lesson 1 you trained one model and took its score at face value. This
lesson asks the two questions a professional asks next: **how complex should
a model be**, and **which model should you deploy?**

You will watch a decision tree reach a perfect score on its training data
and then do *worse than guessing* on new learners. You will use
cross-validation to choose a model without peeking at the test set. Then the
ethics lab compares five models on fairness — and the answer to "does a
better model treat people more fairly?" is not the one you expect.

> ⏳ *The model-comparison cell trains a 100-tree random forest, so it takes
> a few seconds in the browser.*

## Checkpoint

- What does it look like when a model **overfits** — and why did the
  unlimited tree score worse than always predicting "pass"?
- Why must the **test set** play no part in choosing your model?
- Three models were within about a point of each other on accuracy. Were
  they also tied on fairness?
- For learners without a laptop, how did every trained model compare with
  simply predicting "pass" for everyone? What does that tell you?
- The EU AI Act asks for an "appropriate level of accuracy". Whose accuracy?
