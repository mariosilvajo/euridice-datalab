The DataLab is where you **learn and practise**. When you want your skills
**formally recognised**, EURIDICE provides the certification layer.

## What is a microcredential?

A small, stand-alone, certified learning unit — defined by the [EU Council
Recommendation on a European Approach to Microcredentials
(2022/C 243/02)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32022H0627%2802%29).
Each one records what you learned (**learning outcomes**), how much work it
took (**ECTS credits** — 1 ECTS ≈ 25–28 hours), and how it was assessed.

## How EURIDICE certifies

EURIDICE's **Microcredential Review and Certification Board** — senior
academics and education experts appointed by the project's General Assembly
— awards a *Quality-Assured MicroCredential* stamp to courses whose design,
outcomes and assessment meet the EU standard.

## How the DataLab fits in

| Stage | Where it happens |
|---|---|
| Learning & practice | **Here** — free, open, no account |
| Self-checked exercises | **Here** — instant feedback in your browser |
| Formal assessment (graded submissions, projects) | Moodle at a EURIDICE partner institution |
| Credential issuance | EURIDICE partner, compatible with **European Digital Credentials for Learning** |

The DataLab's tracks are designed as the hands-on backbone of EURIDICE's
professional training microcredentials (Work Package 4), including **Applied
AI and Machine Learning Fundamentals** (1 ECTS). The open learning here and
the formal assessment there use the same materials — so nothing you learn
is wasted.

## Your portfolio: the bridge between the two

Every ethics lab ends with a written judgment, and the
[capstone](/tracks/ml/03-capstone/) adds a **model card** for a model you
built and audited yourself. The DataLab saves all of it as you go, and
`export_portfolio()` turns it into a single file containing:

- your six written judgments, each labelled with the lesson it came from
- your model card — features, accuracy against the baseline, per-group
  results and your four written sections
- a machine-readable copy of the same record, for platforms that import it

It is **self-reported evidence**, not a credential in itself. You submit it
to a EURIDICE partner institution, where it can be assessed as part of a
microcredential's portfolio component. Nothing is uploaded from the DataLab:
the file is built on your device and you decide where it goes.
